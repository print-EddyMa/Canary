Canary Strata

A colour system for Canary. One object everywhere, three colour families that each mean one thing, and a rule that colour has to be earned by the evidence.

1

One object: the record

Every answer Canary gives is one variable in one region, year by year. It is always drawn the same way: a strip with one cell per year, coloured by how far that year sat from normal. The globe is the same object spread over space.

  • Full size in the verdict, with the trend line through it.
  • Row size in every table and in a place's vital signs.
  • Glyph size in the variable picker and on map pins.
2

Colour means one thing

Three families, one per kind of variable. Each has two poles and a neutral grey middle, so "no change" never looks like a result. Within a family the poles keep their meaning everywhere: on the globe, in the record, in the numbers.

Blue to red and brown to teal follow the conventions of climate assessment maps. Red always points the way a warming world pushes, so less sea ice is red and more frost days are blue.

3

Colour is earned

A claim gets its family colour only when the test says it is unlikely to be chance. Otherwise it stays graphite: the number, the trend line, the map cell. The record itself always shows its colours, because those are measurements, not claims.

4

Progress means more evidence

Bars in Canary fill with evidence, never with decoration. The evidence bar runs from "could easily be chance" to "very unlikely to be chance", with the 5% line marked. When a trend is not settled, the record shows dashed cells for the years still needed at the current rate.

5

Numbers carry their range

A rate is never shown alone. Under it sits its 95% range on an axis with zero marked. If the range crosses zero the number cannot be called, and you can see why without reading a p-value.

6

Yellow means look here

Canary yellow is never data. It marks the one thing to look at: the region you chose, the place you clicked, the finding in the headline, the keyboard focus. Status has its own colours, and each always comes with a shape and a word.

8

What moves

The interface moves only when the world does, or when you asked something. Everything stops for reduced motion.

The globe turnsSlowly, until you touch it. The night side is where it is night now.
WindParticles follow the latest NASA GEOS analysis.
The record fillsYear by year while the data loads, so progress is the record itself.
RipplesWhere you clicked, and where water reaches the sea.
Live eventsA slow pulse on events that are active now. Past events are still.
